# Dev Propulsion Labs

I've recorded 45 episodes so far with the people behind developer tools, in person, in San Francisco. David Cramer of Sentry, Paul Copplestone of Supabase, Sam Lambert of PlanetScale, and DHH have all been on the show. We talk about how they built the product, how they grew it, and how it makes money. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLAgBW0XUpyOUMf8EblYokBiRVOwMnA71H)

## Episodes

- [What William Imoh taught me about AEO, killing your own traffic, and building a GTM agent](/podcast/william-imoh-hackmamba.md) — August 2026 · Hackmamba. William studied chemical engineering and taught himself to code before founding Hackmamba. Why AEO is mostly ten years of good SEO, cutting the developer traffic that would never buy, and replacing seven tools with one agent-powered platform.
- [What Kate Holterhoff taught me about AI slop, agent experience, and the open source crisis](/podcast/kate-holterhoff-redmonk.md) — July 2026 · RedMonk. Kate painted murals and earned a literature PhD before she started covering developer infrastructure at RedMonk. AI slop in open source pull requests, code review as the bottleneck, and agents as a new way developers find tools.
- [What Matt Biilmann taught me about agent experience as the new developer experience](/podcast/matt-biilmann-netlify.md) — May 2026 · Netlify. Matt studied musicology in Copenhagen and later coined the term agent experience (AX). The four pillars of agent-ready products, and his case that the market for builders grows from 17 million developers to three billion people.
- [What Max Prilutskiy taught me about going from a hackathon to YC on cold calls and word of mouth](/podcast/max-prilutskiy-lingodev.md) — April 2026 · Lingo.dev. Max started coding at nine in Belarus and built Lingo.dev out of a hackathon he entered with zero ideas. How cold exploration calls shaped his go-to-market, and why he thinks word of mouth is still the only channel that works.
- [What Amir Rustamzadeh taught me about what VCs really look for in devtools founders](/podcast/amir-rustamzadeh-firestreak.md) — April 2026 · Firestreak Ventures. Amir worked at JPL and SpaceX before moving into venture capital. Why he backs founders before products, what makes a developer tool fundable, and why most never reach venture scale.
- [What Anuraag Gutgutia taught me about trust as the only currency in enterprise AI sales](/podcast/anuraag-gutgutia-truefoundry.md) — March 2026 · TrueFoundry. Anuraag left hedge funds for AI infrastructure. His layered trust framework for enterprise sales, getting through the year before the first big deal, and why he thinks voice agents come next.
- [What Stas Kelvich taught me about Neon's $1B acquisition and the agent gold rush](/podcast/stas-kelvich-neon.md) — March 2026 · Neon. Stas was a quantum physicist before he co-founded Neon, which Databricks acquired for $1B. Serverless Postgres, infrastructure built for agents, and how 90 lawyers closed the deal in 30 days.
- [What Harjot Gill taught me about going viral in Japan and building guardrails for AI code](/podcast/harjot-gill-coderabbit.md) — February 2026 · CodeRabbit. AI code review as the new bottleneck, going viral in Japan on zenn.dev, running product-led growth alongside enterprise sales, and why Harjot thinks the old startup playbooks stopped working.
- [What David Gomes taught me about why half of developers still aren't using AI](/podcast/david-gomes-cursor.md) — February 2026 · Cursor. How differently engineers work inside Cursor, building a 50-person team in Portugal, and going through Neon's $1B Databricks acquisition.
- [What Monica Sarbu taught me about rebuilding a product from scratch and leading with people](/podcast/monica-sarbu-xata.md) — January 2026 · Xata. The question that changed Xata's product direction, rebuilding the platform from scratch, why diverse teams win, and Monica's people-first leadership in Romania and San Francisco.
- [What Piyush Agarwal taught me about why devtools companies don't know their customers](/podcast/piyush-agarwal-reo-dev.md) — January 2026 · Reo.dev. Intent signals that lift response rates from low single digits to 26%, why Piyush calls GTM the hardest unsolved problem in devtools, and a first product built as a Google Sheet.
- [What David Cramer taught me about building for the Fortune 500,000](/podcast/david-cramer-sentry.md) — December 2025 · Sentry. Mass-market pricing, 4M+ developers won through bottom-up adoption alone, David handing off both the CEO and CTO roles, and why he thinks 20-year-olds should stop starting companies.
- [What Abhi Aiyer taught me about making moves in AI tooling](/podcast/abhi-aiyer-mastra.md) — December 2025 · Mastra. TypeScript for AI agents, the "that's cool" pivot moment, a book that went viral and drove growth, and raising from 120+ builder-investors.
- [What Michael Grinich taught me about building invisible infrastructure](/podcast/michael-grinich-workos.md) — November 2025 · WorkOS. The infrastructure behind OpenAI and Cursor, moving upmarket to enterprise, firing the marketing team, and why Michael wants good infrastructure to go unnoticed.
- [What Ivan Burazin taught me about walking away from revenue to bet on agents](/podcast/ivan-burazin-daytona.md) — November 2025 · Daytona. Walking away from $300K ARR on New Year's Eve, composable computers for AI agents, 19 years with the same co-founders, and Ivan's "genius zone" approach to go-to-market.
- [What Paul Copplestone taught me about scaling Supabase to 5M developers with no meetings](/podcast/paul-copplestone-supabase.md) — November 2025 · Supabase. Hiring ex-founders with beaten-down egos, why Paul doesn't think vibe coding is a bubble, how Launch Weeks took off by accident, and building a database company meant to last 30 years.
- [What Zeno Rocha taught me about building work so good your heroes want to copy it](/podcast/zeno-rocha-resend.md) — October 2025 · Resend. An $18M Series A, a 20K waitlist built on Zeno's founder brand, cutting scope without cutting quality, and Resend's jab-jab-right-hook open source strategy.
- [What Sam Lambert taught me about databases that never go down](/podcast/sam-lambert-planetscale.md) — October 2025 · PlanetScale. Staying up through the AWS US-East outage, growing from 750 users to hundreds of thousands of databases, taste as a competitive advantage, and why vibe-coded databases are a different market.
- [What Jeff Huber taught me about context engineering and why RAG is brain rot](/podcast/jeff-huber-chroma.md) — September 2025 · Chroma. Context engineering over retrieval, what million-token windows don't fix, Chroma's engine-vs-car open source strategy, and design as a moat in AI infrastructure.
- [What José Valim taught me about building a language from curiosity](/podcast/jose-valim-elixir.md) — June 2025 · Elixir. 13 years of Elixir, choosing sound technical decisions over adoption trends, a decentralized community, and José's work on AI tooling with Tidewave.
- [What DHH taught me about staying unconventional for 20 years](/podcast/dhh-rails.md) — December 2023 · Ruby on Rails / Basecamp. 20 years of Rails, leaving the cloud, bootstrapping without VC, opinionated software, and what DHH's racing career taught him about running companies.
