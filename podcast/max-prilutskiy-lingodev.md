# What Max Prilutskiy taught me about going from a hackathon to YC on cold calls and word of mouth

*Dev Propulsion Labs · April 2026 · Guest: Max Prilutskiy, CEO and co-founder of Lingo.dev · Host: Victoria Melnikova*

Max Prilutskiy is the CEO and co-founder of Lingo.dev, an AI-powered localization platform. He began coding at nine in Belarus, sold a bootstrapped startup before this one, and started Lingo.dev at a hackathon with zero ideas. What I loved about our conversation is how much of his go-to-market came from getting on calls with people and listening.

## Coding since nine, and a first exit before Lingo.dev

I'm Victoria Melnikova, and I have a soft spot for founders who started young. Max was coding at nine in Belarus, and by the time he got to Lingo.dev he'd already built and sold a bootstrapped startup. You can hear it in how he works: he doesn't cling to any one idea, and he's comfortable shipping before the plan is finished. In my experience, founders with an exit behind them trust discovery more than their first idea, and Max fits that pattern.

## A hackathon with zero ideas

I keep repeating this detail to the founders I work with: Lingo.dev started at a hackathon where Max had no idea what to build. He showed up and made something, and the AI localization platform grew out of that low-stakes setting. Plenty of founders believe great companies start with a singular insight. Sometimes they start with a weekend and a willingness to try.

## Cold exploration calls as a whole go-to-market

Max's go-to-market is the part closest to my own work. He built it out of cold exploration calls, where the goal is to understand the person on the other end rather than to close them, and those calls shaped both what Lingo.dev became and how he talks about it. I spend a lot of my time pushing early founders to do this, and I still rarely meet one who made customer discovery the engine of their strategy.

## Why word of mouth is still the only channel that works

For Max, word of mouth is still the only channel that reliably works. That matches what I see with developer-facing products: developers trust other developers, and no amount of paid acquisition substitutes for a peer saying "use this." For an early devtools company, the product and the first relationships do the marketing, and everything else amplifies them.

## What agent experience means for devtools in 2026

We closed on where developer tools are heading and what agent experience means in 2026. Localization is a good place to watch that from. It sits between code, content, and the people reading that content in different languages, and agents are starting to show up in exactly that kind of workflow. Max started Lingo.dev at a hackathon and got it into YC on cold calls and word of mouth, and he reminded me that the fundamentals of building for developers haven't changed as much as the tooling around them.
